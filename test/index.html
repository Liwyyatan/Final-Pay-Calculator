Final pay calculator

A clear view of your end-of-contract package under Portuguese labour rules, with an estimate of Social Security and IRS for each parcel. It updates as you type.

How every amount is calculated

The amounts

Monthly base salary
Annual pay ÷ 14. If your annual pay covers only 12 payments, without the holiday and Christmas bonuses, it is ÷ 12 instead. If you tick the shift allowance box, 15% is added, and every amount below uses that higher monthly pay.
Severance for years of service
Monthly base salary ÷ 30 gives one day of pay. Multiply it by the severance days per year (14 + 16 by default) and by the years of service. Part-years count proportionally.
Additional severance
The fixed amount you enter, added as it is.
Unused vacation
Vacation days left × the value of one day. One day is monthly pay × 12 ÷ 260.
Unused training hours
Training hours owed × the hourly rate. The hourly rate is base salary × 12 ÷ (52 × weekly working hours).
Holiday bonus
One month of base salary × months worked this year ÷ 12, minus anything already paid. Months run from 1 January, or from the contract start if it is later, to the contract end.
Christmas bonus
Calculated the same way as the holiday bonus.
Annual bonus
The amount you enter, paid in full.
Before deductions
All the amounts above added together.

The deductions

Social Security
11% of the amounts that count as pay: unused vacation, holiday bonus, Christmas bonus and annual bonus. Nothing is taken from the severance or from the training hours.
IRS on the severance
Severance is tax-free up to your average monthly regular pay (base salary × 14 ÷ 12) × years of service. Only the part above that limit is taxed. You can also take the additional severance out of IRS and Social Security with the checkbox.
IRS on everything else
Estimated with the 2026 Portuguese withholding table. The holiday and Christmas bonuses are taxed on their own. Vacation, training, the annual bonus and any taxed severance are added on top of the month’s salary, so they are taxed at a higher rate. Dependents and the disability factor lower the result. It is an advance, and the final tax is settled in the annual return.
Lawyer’s fee
A fixed 2.5% of the total before deductions.
After deductions
Before deductions − Social Security − IRS − lawyer’s fee.

    How the deductions are estimated

    Social Security is the employee’s 11% on pay items, except the unused training hours. Severance from a collective dismissal is outside the Social Security base, so it carries no contribution. If you tick the option to take the additional severance out of IRS and Social Security, it gets no IRS. The lawyer’s fee still applies to the total of the whole package.

    IRS follows the 2026 mainland withholding tables (Despacho 233-A/2026). The holiday and Christmas bonuses are withheld on their own. Vacation pay, training pay, the annual bonus and any taxable severance are added to the month’s salary, which is assumed to be paid in the same payment, so they are withheld at a higher rate.

    The severance is exempt from IRS up to average monthly regular pay × years of service. The exemption is lost, for example, if the person works for the same employer again within 24 months.

    The lawyer’s fee is a fixed 2.5% of the total of the whole package and does not change the Social Security or IRS estimate.

    For each dependent with a disability of 60% or more, €84.82 a month (€42.41 if married with two incomes) is added to the amount deducted from the withholding. Your employer can multiply it by up to 3, or up to 6 if married with two incomes, and the calculator uses the factor you enter.

    Not covered: the Azores and Madeira tables, the tables for taxpayers who have a disability themselves, IRS Jovem, and the separate exemption for voluntary performance bonuses. Withholding is an advance, and the final tax is settled in the annual return.

    Estimates only, not legal or tax advice. The three-month minimum and the statutory caps in article 366 of the Labour Code are not applied. The days per year are applied to the whole length of service, without the transitional split for service before 1 May 2023.
